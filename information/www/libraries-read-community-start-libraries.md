# Read in Community - Start at the Libraries

Although reading can be a rewarding solitary pursuit, it’s also a perfect activity for socializing and building community. The College’s Faculty-Staff Book Groups are a wonderful illustration of the power of reading as a social activity. This popular initiative, organized by Special Assistant for Presidential Initiatives Pam Shropshire and Associate Professor of French and Executive Director of Global Engagement Carina Yervasi, brings together professors and staff members from across the college to read for pleasure and connect beyond the bounds of their jobs.

This year, the Faculty-Staff Book Group theme is Philadelphia; past themes have included climate, food, work, and science fiction. You can find a full list of Philly-centric books folks are reading below, with links to order copies through the library. Dozens of people are participating in the program–there’s even a wait list. But if you were not able to participate this year, or if the College program sparked your love for book groups, you can start a book club of your own, and the Libraries can help. Here are some ways to get started.

- Browse our community-curated summer reading guide to find recommended reads.
- Search for your book in Tripod, the Libraries’ catalog.
  - Tripod automatically searches all the Libraries in the Trico, so you can easily order books not just from Swarthmore but also Haverford and Bryn Mawr. That means there are often multiple copies of a book to go around.
- If you’re reading an older book, consider having group members compare different editions. For example, there are over 70 different editions of Pride and Prejudice across the Trico. Your club members will enjoy it most ardently!
- Include e-books and audiobooks as an option. Searching a book by title or author in Tripod will bring up digital editions, or you can browse Overdrive, our home for electronic pleasure reading.

Carina and Pam also have some advice for running a successful book club:

- Invite people who genuinely like to read and who are willing to take a chance and be adventurous.
- If you have a narrow taste in reading, a general book club can broaden your horizons, while a niche book club can play to your interests.
- Start with some ground rules. For example, participants must finish the book, or conversation should only be about literature.
- If you find a book in the Libraries’ collections, that’s a good indication of quality–it means a librarian or another community member deemed it of value to the community.

Whether you join a book club or start your own, the Libraries can help you build community, one page at a time.
