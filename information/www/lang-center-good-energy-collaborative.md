# The Good Energy Collaborative

## Projects and Recent Work

### Solar Futures: Youth Leadership at the Intersections of EJ and STEM

Solar Futures is a rework of the GEC's previous youth STEM programming, Solar Agriculture. Solar Futures is designed to introduce high schoolers and young adults to topics focused on community engagement, environmental justice, and STEM, with a focus on circuits and solar. Our Swarthmore Student GEC team designs a hands-on and fun 8-week co-learning curriculum to engage youth and build their confidence with STEM and leadership skills. At the end of the program, students are able to design and build a solar installation for their community. Students learn how to make a change in their community through STEM, engagement, and social justice.

### Youth Council: Leadership and Activism in Philly and Chester

The Youth Council is a collection of high school students in both Philly and Chester coming together to discuss, learn, and create change in their community. The Youth Council focuses on leadership development skills and activism through youth-led conversations and youth-planned events. These conversations and events include creating a podcast where youth were able to have meaningful conversations with community leaders, going to Swarthmore College to paint and discuss their visionary future for themselves and their community, going to Philadelphia City Hall to advocate for housing justice, and hosting a block party to celebrate the community and its people. With weekly meetings and monthly events, the youth dedicate time to bettering the community and themselves.

### Environmental Justice Walks: Highlighting EJ Communities and Sites

The GEC, with our community partners, was able to host Environmental Justice Walks throughout neighborhoods in Chester, West Philly, and Hunting Park. These walks are designed to stop at sites that show the challenges the communities face, but also highlight the good work communities, organizations, and people are doing. The GEC hosts EJ Tours in Chester with CRCQL to show the hardships the community faces with America's largest incinerator, Reworld, in the middle of their community. At the West Philly Walk, community members were able to design and paint a community mural at the Reentry Community Farm. And at the Hunting Park Walk, attendees were able to reflect on the sites throughout the walk and imagine how to build an even better community.

### Community Classrooms: Intergenerational Co-learning Spaces

Community classrooms teach and create a co-learning space for community members of all ages to discuss topics that traditional classrooms do not teach. We discuss topics that community members place an emphasis on and feel that there is a gap in knowledge. Everyone is invited and encouraged to share their opinions and thoughts, no matter the age. The topics discussed include farming in the off-season, book talks, composting, and the importance of urban farms. Open discussions allow community members to gain knowledge and share knowledge, which creates a more active and mindful community.

### Solar Agriculture Labs: Youth Leadership Through the Intersections of EJ and STEM

The Solar Agriculture Labs are the Good Energy Collaborative’s space for young people to step into environmental justice and STEM. Designed as foundational experiences, these labs help students build the confidence and skills needed to become changemakers in their own neighborhoods. Hosted in community spaces like YEAH Philly and PSA's Healing Station, the labs are more than just hands-on learning. They’re about co-creation and co-learning. Local young adults, students, and local partners come together to explore critical environmental issues, co-design solutions, and dream of better futures. Through STEM-based activities, artistic expression, and conversations with local activists, students learn that environmental change isn’t out of reach—it starts with them.

### School Visits: Bringing Environmental Advocacy to the Classroom

The Good Energy Collaborative brings grassroots environmental leadership directly into classrooms across Philadelphia and Chester. Our school visits connect students with stories of bottom-up change, highlighting ethical, community-rooted solutions to environmental issues. In collaboration with teachers, we link classroom content to real-world environmental justice work. Students hear directly from local activists and gain insight into how they can be stewards of their own environments, empowered to make a difference in their schools, neighborhoods, and beyond.

### Environmental Justice Labs: Leadership and Advocacy in Chester

In partnership with Chester Education Foundation and CRCQL, our Environmental Justice Labs work directly with young adults in the Chester Upland School District. The labs focuses on building awareness of environmental justice, preparing students for college, and sharing tools to become powerful advocates in their own community. At the heart of this work is CHOICES—the youth hub of CRCQL—where students learn about the ongoing challenges in Chester and how they can lead the change. The program also introduces them to "Chester Stars", inspiring a new generation of environmentally conscious leaders who are informed, equipped, and ready to act.

### Community Fireside Chats: Connecting, Healing, and Building Power Together

Our community gatherings—Solar & Your Community and Fireside Chats—are powerful spaces for residents to connect, learn, and reflect. In partnership with grassroots organizations, we introduce solar energy programming, including solar installation courses, energy efficiency resources, and local advocacy efforts. These gatherings aren’t just about information—they’re about healing, building relationships, and sharing knowledge. Together, we create spaces where community members can feel empowered, supported, and connected in the transition toward sustainable, people-powered energy solutions.
