# RIAs World, Jules Cruz, '27

Library Resources You Might Not Know About

Many students think of Swarthmore’s libraries primarily as places to find course readings or a quiet study space. But the libraries also offer an entire network of guides, experts, and collections designed to encourage students’ exploration of ideas. Here are three resources I feel are particularly invaluable.

## Tri-Co’s Research Guides

Accessible from Tripod, this page is a collection of research resources for every possible Tri-Co major. These resources are carefully chosen by our librarians to help students gain a reliable foothold in their subject areas of interest. My personal favorite of these guides is the Topic Introductions page, which is a curated list of websites like Aeon or The Conversation which present scholarly research in ways much more digestible for wider audiences. They’re perfect for getting a taste of what certain research topics entail and sparking curiosity about different subject areas, which comes in handy when brainstorming for theses or other large research projects.

## Subject Specialist Librarians

Often, what helps students most isn’t just access to expansive databases, but guidance from someone who knows how best to use those databases to research a given subject. Every Swarthmore major has its own subject librarian associated with it — someone with deep experience with the literature and research methods of the discipline(s) they specialize in. You can book a one-on-one consultation with these subject specialists and receive help framing research questions, finding the most relevant scholarly sources, or locating sources you might not otherwise have even thought to look for.

## Special Collections

I see a lot of students overlook Special Collections as a resource while doing research because what they’re studying isn’t directly related to the college’s history, peace studies, or Quakerism. But the materials in Special Collections are far more extensive than most think; for instance, students can go to Friends Historical to research anything from mycology to architecture to LGBTQ+ rights activism in the ‘80s.

These resources aren’t just for research-heavy majors. They’re for any student who wants to have an easier time starting papers, choosing topics, or finding sources that actually give you the information you’re looking for. Beyond the resources I discussed here, Swarthmore’s library ecosystem offers countless time- and frustration-saving tools that aren’t restricted to specific majors or classes. So if you’re ever feeling stuck or simply curious, I encourage you to see what the library has to offer you.
