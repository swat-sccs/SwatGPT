# Teaching

#### ESCH Courses

The Lang Center works with faculty and the Registrar’s Office to designate certain courses as Engaged Scholarship, or “ESCH” courses. Engaged scholarship—a term popularized by Ernest Boyer— connects “the rich resources of the university to our most pressing social, civic, and ethical problems” (1996). Engaged scholars direct their energies not solely toward an academic community, but also toward pressing public issues or shared problems.

Swarthmore College identifies ESCH courses as those that:

- address a contemporary issue of pressing public concern;
- build knowledge about how to ameliorate social problems;
- engage with various publics and/or include a community-based learning component.

You can view current ESCH courses via our Engaged Scholarship Map.

Faculty who are unsure whether or not their course would or could qualify for the ESCH designation are encouraged to reach out to Katie Price or another member of the Lang Center team.

#### Engaged Scholarship Teaching Grant (ESTG)

ESTGs recognize that teaching ESCH courses often requires extra resources because of their focus on issues/problems of interest to a community outside of Swarthmore College, along with an attempt to analyze *and* address those issues with some external community. 

Applications are **due annually on May 1**  for courses running the following academic year, though serendipity requests are considered after **January 10** of each year (for Spring) and on a rolling basis.
