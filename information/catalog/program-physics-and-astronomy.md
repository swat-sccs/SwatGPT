The Physics and Astronomy Department teaches the concepts and methods that lead to an understanding of the fundamental laws governing the physical universe.

Emphasis is placed on quantitative, analytical reasoning, as distinct from the mere acquisition of facts. Particular importance is also attached to laboratory work because physics and astronomy are experimental and observational sciences.

Involvement in research is a major component in the education of scientists. The department offers a number of opportunities for students to participate in original research projects, conducted by members of the faculty, on campus.

Department faculty conduct research on materials for renewable energy, granular media, liquid crystals, plasma, physics education, and in observational, theoretical, and computational astrophysics and cosmology.

The department operates the Peter van de Kamp Observatory for student and faculty research, plus several small telescopes for instructional use. The observatory is equipped with a 61-cm reflecting telescope, a high-resolution spectrograph, and a CCD camera for imaging and photometry. A monthly visitors' night at the observatory is announced on the department website.

Additional information is available at <https://www.swarthmore.edu/physics-astronomy>.

## The Academic Program

In order to receive a degree from Swarthmore as a physics, astrophysics, or astronomy major, a student must complete the requirements described below. This document provides some [sample plans of study](https://drive.google.com/file/d/17xfnn7e6Ns6zbI1Mgui20I-OCUJ-NlHO/view?usp=sharing).  
  
In the Physics and Astronomy Department, many junior and senior level offerings are taught in a seminar format. All prospective majors and minors in the department should realize this when planning programs. The seminars are open to all students, both honors and course majors and minors.

### First Course Recommendations

[ PHYS 003. General Physics I: Motion, Forces, and Energy](https://catalog.swarthmore.edu/preview_course_nopop.php?catoid=32&coid=110758): Motion, Forces, and Energy is the normal entry point to all three majors in our department and is also recommended for engineering students. It is the first semester of a two-semester calculus-based survey of classical physics.

[ PHYS 003L. General Physics I: Motion, Forces, and Energy with Biological and Medical Applications](https://catalog.swarthmore.edu/preview_course_nopop.php?catoid=32&coid=111229) is the entry point for a two-semester calculus-based sequence designed specifically to make connections between physics and the life sciences for biology, biochemistry, neuroscience, and pre-medical students. It covers broadly the same physics topics as PHYS 003. If a student in PHYS 003L becomes interested in majoring or minoring in physics, PHYS 003L may be substituted for PHYS 003 after consulting with the department chair.

We offer a physics placement test for students who have taken high school physics, to determine whether they should take PHYS 003 or if their background is appropriate for them to place out and begin in PHYS 005. The placement test is made available to all incoming first-year students over the summer before matriculation. Students interested in physics, astronomy, astrophysics, or engineering who have taken physics in high school and have a solid foundation in solving problems with calculus are urged to take the placement test before the specified summer deadline. Note that the test can only be taken once so students should review before taking the test. Students who wish to take the test for the first time later in their time at Swarthmore should email physics@swarthmore.edu to arrange to take the test, usually during the break between semesters or the summer. By College policy, students seeking pre-matriculation credit on their transcript for PHYS 003 must provide an AP score of 5 on the Physics C-Mechanics test, or an IB physics score of 6 or 7, as well as a suitable placement test score. (Consult placement test information to determine whether the placement test is needed in addition to the AP  
score.) Placement does not require an AP or IB score, only credit.

[PHYS 005. The World of Particles and Waves](https://catalog.swarthmore.edu/preview_course_nopop.php?catoid=32&coid=110761) introduces and explores in some depth optics and quantum mechanics - two key theories of modern physics and astronomy. It also welcomes non-majors interested in learning this material at a moderately mathematical level. For non-majors interested in a less mathematical course, Astronomy 1 is recommended.

In general, majors cannot replace Physics 005 (or most other major requirements) with AP credit or college courses taken during high school.

### Core Programs

In the spirit of a liberal arts education, we believe that a physics, astrophysics, or astronomy major can be beneficial and stimulating to students with a wide range of long-term interests and goals. The physics curriculum and the astronomy curriculum listed below both provide excellent training in quantitative reasoning and independent problem solving, skills that are applicable in a wide variety of arenas (law, medicine, science journalism, public policy). Since all of the fundamental areas are covered, the physics curriculum is also excellent preparation for a career in a scientific field related to physics, such as engineering or teaching physics in high school. The astronomy curriculum is excellent preparation for teaching astronomy at the high school level, or working as a telescope operator or data analyst. Many students in our department do double majors.

Students considering PhD work in any of physics, astrophysics, and astronomy are encouraged to take more advanced courses and seminars than the minimum required and to consult with faculty about which advanced work is most relevant for their particular interests. For students considering PhD work in astronomy, the astrophysics major is normally recommended.

## Applying for Majors

A student applying to become a course or honors major in physics or astronomy should have completed or be completing at least two of [PHYS 003](https://catalog.swarthmore.edu/preview_course_nopop.php?catoid=32&coid=110758) , [PHYS 004](https://catalog.swarthmore.edu/preview_course_nopop.php?catoid=32&coid=110759), [PHYS 005](https://catalog.swarthmore.edu/preview_course_nopop.php?catoid=32&coid=110761), and [PHYS 006](https://catalog.swarthmore.edu/preview_course_nopop.php?catoid=32&coid=112389), and the accompanying math courses. A student applying for the astrophysics major should also normally have completed [ASTR 016](https://catalog.swarthmore.edu/preview_course_nopop.php?catoid=32&coid=109882) (or [ASTR 014](https://catalog.swarthmore.edu/preview_course_nopop.php?catoid=32&coid=111659), although it is not required for the astrophysics major). To be accepted as a course major, the applicant must have earned grades of C+ or better in physics, astronomy, and math courses, and have demonstrated the independent learning skills needed to succeed in the upper level curriculum. To be accepted as an honors major, the applicant must normally have an average grade in physics, astronomy, and math courses of B or better.

## Research Opportunities

### Independent Work

Physics, astrophysics, and astronomy majors are encouraged to undertake independent research projects, especially in the senior year, either in conjunction with one of the senior seminars, or as a special project for separate credit (PHYS/ASTR 094). Members of the physics or astronomy faculty are willing to suggest possible projects and to supervise one of these if the student chooses to pursue it. Students completing work under PHYS/ASTR 094 are required to submit final written and oral reports of their work to the department. In preparation for independent experimental work, prospective physics majors are strongly urged to take the required 0.5 credit course PHYS 063, Procedures in Experimental Physics, during their fall semester of their sophomore year, which will qualify them to work in the departmental shops. There are usually many opportunities for students to receive financial support to work with faculty members on research projects during the summer.

### Thesis

Students may do a theoretical or experimental research thesis representing the results of independent work done under the supervision of a faculty member. This thesis will usually cover work begun in the summer after the junior year and completed during the senior year. A thesis is optional for all students in the Honors Program.

## Study Abroad

With proper planning, study away from Swarthmore for one or two semesters is possible while majoring in physics, astronomy, or astrophysics. However, the sequential nature of the Physics and Astronomy curricula makes careful planning for study abroad a necessity. The important point is to begin planning at an early stage. This allows students (1) to make sure courses not available abroad are taken at Swarthmore, and (2) to find out well in advance what physics and astronomy courses are available in the various study abroad programs. With careful planning, it is completely feasible to complete a physics major without taking physics abroad (e.g. if one is studying in a non-English-speaking country).

### Physics Seminars

There are four one-credit physics seminars offered on a regular basis, regardless of faculty leaves. These are [PHYS 107](https://catalog.swarthmore.edu/preview_course_nopop.php?catoid=32&coid=112391), [PHYS 111](https://catalog.swarthmore.edu/preview_course_nopop.php?catoid=32&coid=110773), [PHYS 112](https://catalog.swarthmore.edu/preview_course_nopop.php?catoid=32&coid=110774), and [PHYS 114](https://catalog.swarthmore.edu/preview_course_nopop.php?catoid=32&coid=110776). In addition, one or two one-credit elective physics seminars are offered each year. 

  * PHYS 107. Quantum Mechanics.
  * PHYS 111. Analytical Dynamics
  * PHYS 112. Electrodynamics
  * PHYS 114. Statistical Physics
  * PHYS 115. Advanced Quantum Mechanics
  * PHYS 130. General Relativity
  * PHYS 131. Particle Physics
  * PHYS 132. Nonlinear Dynamics.
  * PHYS 135. Condensed Matter Physics
  * PHYS 137. Cosmological Physics
  * PHYS 138. Plasma Physics
  * PHYS 139. Biophysics
  * PHYS 180. Honors Thesis
